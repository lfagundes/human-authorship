```text
Not legal advice, but the main legal issues are:

- **Human authorship:** In the U.S., copyright generally protects only human-authored expression. Pure machine output may be uncopyrightable.
- **Scope of protection:** Even if AI generated parts of the code, human-written architecture, selection, arrangement, edits, refactoring, tests, comments, and integration may be protectable.
- **Prompting alone may be insufficient:** The U.S. Copyright Office has said prompts alone usually do not give enough creative control over the final expressive output.
- **Registration disclosure:** If registering copyright, AI-generated material may need to be disclosed and excluded/disclaimed, while claiming only the human-authored portions.
- **Evidence of human contribution:** Keep prompts, drafts, diffs, commits, design notes, review comments, tests, and records showing human judgment and modification.
- **Ownership vs copyrightability:** A tool’s terms may assign output rights to you, but contract ownership does not guarantee the output is copyrightable.
- **Third-party infringement risk:** AI output can resemble copyrighted code or include licensed/open-source snippets; you still need provenance, license, and similarity review.
- **Open-source license contamination:** Generated code may accidentally reproduce GPL/AGPL/etc. code, creating compliance issues if copied substantially.
- **Work-for-hire/employment:** If employees use assistants, employer ownership depends on employment agreements, contractor assignments, and tool terms.
- **Trade secrets/confidentiality:** Sharing proprietary code with assistants may waive secrecy or breach client/security obligations unless the tool terms and settings protect data.
- **Patent/invention issues:** AI-assisted code may implement patentable ideas, but inventorship still generally requires human inventors.
- **Jurisdiction differences:** U.S. law is strict on human authorship; UK law has a special rule for some computer-generated works, so the answer can differ by country.

**Practical way to establish human authorship:**

- Record the human design decisions before generation.
- Commit AI output separately from human edits where feasible.
- Preserve assistant prompts and responses as development records.
- Add human review notes explaining why code was accepted, changed, or rejected.
- Refactor, test, integrate, and document the code through human judgment.
- For registration, claim only the human-authored expression and disclose AI-generated portions.

**References:**
```
